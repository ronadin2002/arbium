# Arbium

**Arbium** /ˈɑːr.bi.əm/ — *noun, Neo-Latin*

> The living memory of a tree — the tissue where every season, every wound, every burst of sunlight is encoded into permanent record.

## Etymology

From Latin **arbor** (*tree*) + **cambium** (*the living layer that records each ring of growth*).

The word **arbium** is a Neo-Latin coinage blending two classical Latin roots:

- **arbor** (Latin: *tree*) — the foundational structure, rooted and enduring
- **cambium** (Latin: *exchange, change*) — the thin generative layer in trees responsible for producing new growth rings. In botany, the cambium is the living tissue that records the tree's entire history in its rings.

Together, **arbium** captures the idea of a *tree that remembers* — a living archive that grows richer with each passing season.

## Full Definition

The living memory of a tree — the tissue where every season, every wound, every burst of sunlight is encoded into permanent record. Unlike dead heartwood, the arbium is alive: it grows, adapts, and writes new history while preserving everything that came before.

By extension: a system that doesn't just store the past but **grows from it** — layering new context over old, turning raw experience into something structural, enduring, and alive.
